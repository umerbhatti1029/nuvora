How Much Electricity Does a Slow Cooker Use for 8 Hours?

See how much electricity a slow cooker uses during an eight-hour cooking period and estimate the cost at different U.S. electricity rates.

Published August 16, 2026 · Nuvora editorial guide

An eight-hour slow-cooker cycle can use less electricity than the long run time might suggest. The important number is average power consumption, not simply the maximum rating printed on the appliance.

Quick answerA slow cooker averaging 150W for 8 hours uses 1.2 kWh. At 16¢/kWh, that is about $0.19.

How to calculate electricity use

Energy (kWh) = watts × hours ÷ 1,000

For 150W over eight hours: 150 × 8 ÷ 1,000 = 1.2 kWh. At $0.16/kWh, the cost is about 19 cents.

Why rated wattage is not always actual use

A slow cooker heats up and cycles its heating element to maintain temperature. Its average draw over a complete cooking session can therefore be lower than a constant maximum-wattage estimate.

Slow cooker versus oven

The fairest comparison is total kWh used for the same cooking task. An oven often has a higher rated power, but its heating elements cycle too.

Ways to keep electricity use reasonable

  • Use a cooker size suited to the amount of food.
  • Keep the lid closed unless the recipe requires opening it.
  • Use the recommended cooking time and setting.
  • Use a plug-in meter when you need an exact measurement.

Frequently asked questions

Does a slow cooker use a lot of electricity for 8 hours?

Its use depends on average power draw. A 150W average draw for eight hours is 1.2 kWh.

How much does a 150W slow cooker cost for 8 hours?

At 16¢/kWh, it uses 1.2 kWh and costs about 19 cents.

Is the wattage printed on a slow cooker its exact electricity use?

Not necessarily. Heating elements can cycle, so actual average consumption can be lower.

Does LOW use less electricity than HIGH?

It can, but the exact difference depends on the model and control system.
