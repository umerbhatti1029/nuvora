How Much Does It Cost to Run a Fan Overnight?

Calculate the electricity cost of running a fan overnight using wattage, hours, and your electricity rate, with practical U.S. examples.

Published August 16, 2026 · Nuvora editorial guide

Leaving a fan on while you sleep is common. The electricity cost can be estimated from three things: power draw, hours of use, and your electricity price.

Quick answerA 75-watt fan running for 8 hours uses 0.60 kWh. At 16¢/kWh, that is about $0.10 per night, or $2.88 over 30 nights.

How to calculate the cost

Use this formula:

Cost = (watts ÷ 1,000) × hours × electricity rate

For a 75W fan used for eight hours: 0.075 × 8 = 0.60 kWh. At $0.16/kWh, 0.60 × $0.16 = $0.096.

Does fan speed change electricity use?

It can. The exact difference depends on the motor and controls. If the manufacturer provides wattage for each speed, use the setting you actually use.

Is a fan cheaper than air conditioning?

Usually a fan uses much less electricity than an air conditioner, although they do different jobs. A fan moves air; an air conditioner removes heat. ENERGY STAR notes that ceiling fans cool people rather than rooms.

How to reduce overnight cost

  • Use the lowest comfortable speed.
  • Use a timer if continuous operation is unnecessary.
  • Turn the fan off when the room is empty.
  • Check actual wattage when replacing an older fan.

Frequently asked questions

Is it expensive to leave a fan on all night?

For many household fans, the electricity cost is relatively small. A 75W fan used for eight hours at 16¢/kWh costs about 10 cents.

How much does a 100 watt fan cost for 8 hours?

At 16¢/kWh, a 100W fan uses 0.8 kWh and costs about 13 cents for eight hours.

Does a fan use more electricity on high speed?

It can, but the exact difference depends on the fan and its motor controls.

Is a fan cheaper to run than an air conditioner?

A fan generally uses much less electricity, but it does not perform the same cooling function as an air conditioner.
