How Much Does It Cost to Run an Air Purifier 24/7?

Estimate the electricity cost of running an air purifier continuously, with 20W to 100W examples and different electricity rates.

Published August 16, 2026 · Nuvora editorial guide

Running an air purifier continuously can be useful when consistent filtration is your goal. The electricity cost depends on power draw, operating mode, hours, and your utility rate.

Quick answerA purifier drawing 50W continuously for 24 hours uses 1.2 kWh per day. At 16¢/kWh, that is about $0.19 per day and $5.76 for 30 days.

How to calculate 24/7 cost

Daily cost = (watts ÷ 1,000) × 24 × electricity rate

For 50W at $0.16/kWh: 0.05 × 24 × $0.16 = $0.192 per day.

Does it use the same electricity all day?

Not necessarily. Many units have low, medium, high, sleep, or automatic modes. Automatic controls can change fan speed, so rated maximum wattage is not necessarily average daily consumption.

What changes the real cost?

  • Your electricity rate and billing structure.
  • The operating mode.
  • Whether the motor changes speed or cycles.
  • Actual operating hours.
  • Taxes and fees on your bill.

Ways to reduce electricity use

  • Use an appropriate lower-power or sleep mode when suitable.
  • Use automatic mode when appropriate for the model.
  • Maintain filters according to manufacturer instructions.
  • Measure actual energy use if continuous operation matters.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 50W air purifier cost to run 24/7?

At 16¢/kWh, a constant 50W draw costs about 19 cents per day, $5.76 for 30 days, and $87.60 for 365 days.

Does an air purifier use electricity all the time?

If powered and operating, it uses electricity, but the amount can vary with speed, sleep mode, automatic controls, and cycling.

Is it cheaper to run an air purifier on low?

A lower-power mode generally uses less electricity, but use the setting appropriate for your needs and the manufacturer's instructions.

How can I find exact purifier cost?

Check the model specification and your utility rate; for the most precise result, measure the appliance with a plug-in energy meter.
